## Slide 03 — Fragmented identity prevents coordinated customer activation

```slide-meta
id: fragmented-identity
kind: content
section: strategic-imperative
structure: narrative
```

**Key Message**

Business-unit customer records cannot reliably support cross-business targeting, measurement, or frontline recognition.

**Content**

- Customers may receive overlapping offers from different business units.
- Cross-business behavior is not consistently visible to frontline teams.
- Loyalty value is measured within individual businesses rather than across the customer relationship.

**Speaker Notes**

Clarify that the immediate problem is fragmented recognition, not the absence of a single monolithic loyalty program.

## Slide 04 — Four shared capabilities enable relevant journeys across business units

```slide-meta
id: shared-capabilities
kind: content
section: target-operating-model
structure: grouped-items
```

**Key Message**

A coordinated model requires shared identity, consent, orchestration, and measurement capabilities while allowing business units to retain local relevance.

**Content**

### Shared identity

- Recognize the same customer across business-unit interactions.

### Consent and preferences

- Apply customer permissions consistently across channels and offers.

### Journey orchestration

- Trigger relevant next-best actions across business units.

### Customer-value measurement

- Measure activation and value at the relationship level.

**Key Message**

Central ownership should provide common standards and platforms, while business units retain accountability for proposition design and frontline activation.

**Content**

### Central ownership

- Common journey platform and measurement model

### Business-unit execution

- Frontline activation and customer feedback

## Slide 06 — Delivery should progress from foundation to scaled activation in four phases

```slide-meta
id: delivery-sequence
kind: content
section: delivery-path
structure: sequence
```

**Key Message**

A phased approach reduces delivery risk by proving shared foundations before scaling cross-business journeys.

**Content**

1. Diagnose customer, data, and operating-model gaps.
2. Establish shared identity, consent, and measurement foundations.
3. Pilot priority cross-business journeys in selected customer segments.
4. Scale proven journeys and refine the operating model using measured outcomes.

---

## Slide 07 — The platform separates reusable foundations from customer-facing activation

```slide-meta
id: platform-layers
kind: content
section: enabling-platform
structure: layers
```

**Key Message**

Reusable data and decisioning foundations allow customer-facing teams to activate journeys without rebuilding core capabilities in every business unit.

**Content**

### Customer activation layer

- Channels, frontline tools, offers, and journey experiences

### Decisioning layer

- Segmentation, next-best action, measurement, and attribution

### Foundation layer

- Identity, consent, customer data, and integration services

```slide-meta
id: first-year-roadmap
kind: content
```

**Key Message**

Each delivery phase should have an accountable owner, a measurable outcome, and an explicit evidence gate before investment is expanded.

**Content**

| Phase | Accountable owner | Outcome | Evidence gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnose | Transformation office | Prioritized gaps | Leadership agreement on scope |
| Foundation | Data and technology | Shared identity and consent | Data-quality threshold achieved |
| Pilot | Business-unit leads | Tested priority journeys | Activation uplift validated¹ |
| Scale | Group and business units | Repeatable activation model | Funding decision approved |
